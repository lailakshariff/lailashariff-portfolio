Type comparison

Four sans candidates, one fixed serif

Newsreader is fixed in all four columns — every serif heading here is identical. Only the sans changes, at the same sizes and weights throughout. Instrument Sans is what the site uses now. Manrope is warmer and more distinctive. Schibsted Grotesk was drawn for news products, so it is the one built for long reading and dense small-size UI at the same time. Satoshi is the most geometric of the four and the only one not served from Google Fonts.

Existing AAC tools weren’t built for how young children play

Children with cerebral palsy often experience significant speech and motor limitations, yet most augmentative and alternative communication tools are designed around adult vocabularies and adult motor control. Grid sizes assume precision. Category structures assume literacy. The result is a tool that a therapist can operate but a four-year-old cannot, which pushes the child toward passive use and the caregiver toward abandonment within the first month.

Over eight weeks at United Cerebral Palsy I observed therapy sessions, interviewed speech-language pathologists and occupational therapists, and sat with families during home practice.

What to look at. Read the two long paragraphs in each column first — that block is the heaviest reading on your site, and it is where the three genuinely differ. Then check the 10.5px uppercase chips and the persona card, which is where Instrument Sans gets narrow. Manrope has no italic; Schibsted Grotesk does. Both run a taller x-height than Instrument Sans, so 16.5px in either reads closer to 17.5px and body copy could come down half a pixel. Manrope carries more personality in the a, g and k; Schibsted Grotesk is quieter and holds its shape better at small sizes; Satoshi is the most geometric and the most neutral of the three candidates. One practical note on Satoshi: it loads from Fontshare rather than Google Fonts, so it adds a second font host to every page.
